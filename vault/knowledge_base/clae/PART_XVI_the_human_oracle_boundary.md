# Part XVI — The Human Oracle Boundary

## 1. Purpose

Every Part so far has answered a limitation with a mechanism: a reference where a criterion was
self-authored, an instrument where observation was missing, a floor where distance could not
enforce. This Part addresses the residue — **the properties a system structurally cannot verify
about itself, no matter how good its instruments become.**

Its central claim is that declaring that set is a quality mechanism rather than an admission of
weakness, for the same reason Part IX §5's measurement debt is: an undeclared incapacity is
indistinguishable from a covered dimension, and reads as coverage.

The Part states the general form of the self-verification limit, sharpens Part III's boundary test
into four recognizable marks, establishes that the boundary fails expensively in *both* directions
and that only one of those failures is visible, and fixes who counts as an oracle for a given
question.

## 2. The self-verification limit

Three failures from earlier Parts share one structure.

- Part I: the criterion was authored by the party being assessed.
- Part IV §6: the reference was generated by the assessing system.
- Part IX §5: the measurement scope was chosen from inside the loop.

Each was treated locally, with a local remedy. The general form is:

> **A system cannot verify a property whose criterion it would have to author.**

This is not a capability gap that a better instrument closes. An instrument answers a question
against a criterion; where the criterion itself is what is in question, a more capable instrument
produces a more confident answer to the wrong thing. The limit is structural, and the only escape is
a judgment from outside — which is what an oracle is.

## 3. Four marks of an oracle question

Part III §7's test asks whether a sufficiently capable procedure could answer the question in
principle. That test is correct and "in principle" carries too much weight to be usable at a
decision point. Four marks make it recognizable.

**Value-laden.** The answer depends on what *should* be preferred rather than on what *is the case*.
Whether reliability outranks capability for this artifact is not a fact about the artifact.

**Constituency-dependent.** The answer belongs to whoever bears the consequence. Whether this is
good enough is a question about the bearer's threshold, and the system does not contain it.

**Reference-absent by nature.** No external instance can exist, because the thing is novel or
singular. Part IV §9 permits *undefined* here; an oracle can supply an ordinal judgment where no
measurement is available.

**Self-referential.** The question is about the system's own judgment, so the system's answer is the
very thing in question. *Are your criteria the right criteria?* is unanswerable from inside by
construction — and it is this family's own founding question, which is why CLAE could not have been
derived by the stack evaluating itself.

Any one mark makes it an oracle question. Absent all four, it is an instrument question, and routing
it to a human is an unnecessary cost with a compounding consequence per §5.

## 4. What an oracle is not

**Not "a human."** Part III §7 fixed this: a person running a comparison is operating an instrument.
The same person judging whether the result is acceptable is acting as an oracle. The distinction is
the nature of the question, never the identity of the answerer.

**Not an escalation path.** A hard problem is not thereby an oracle question. Difficulty and
standing are unrelated, and routing hard instrument questions upward is how the channel fills with
work that a procedure should have done.

**Not an approval step.** Approval is authority over an action. An oracle answers a question about a
property. They frequently involve the same person and they are different transactions, and
conflating them means the judgment is never actually recorded — only the permission is.

> **An oracle is a source of judgment the system has no standing to make.** Standing is the
> operative word.

## 5. The boundary fails in both directions

**Too narrow** — too few questions treated as oracle questions. The system answers what it has no
standing to answer: self-certification, Part I in its most direct form. The output looks like every
other measurement and is wrong in a way nothing internal can detect.

**Too wide** — too many questions routed outward. Mechanical questions consume a scarce channel
until answers stop being considered carefully, and then the genuine oracle questions receive that
same degraded attention. **Over-asking destroys the channel it was meant to protect**, and the
damage lands on exactly the questions that had no alternative.

The two failures are not symmetric in visibility, and this is what makes the boundary drift.

| | Too narrow | Too wide |
|---|---|---|
| How it fails | Self-certification | Oracle exhaustion |
| Visibility | **Silent** — the answers look normal | **Loud** — people say they are being over-consulted |
| Feedback | None | Immediate |

Only one direction generates complaints, so systems correct in one direction and drift in the other.
**A boundary left to feedback will always drift narrow**, which is the direction whose failure is
Part I. Placement must therefore be deliberate and reviewed, never tuned by whoever objects.

## 6. The declared set

The mechanism is declaration, and it is the same move this family makes throughout: convert an
invisible incapacity into a visible one.

An undeclared limit is indistinguishable from a covered dimension. Nothing in the reports
distinguishes *we verified this* from *we could not have verified this and did not notice*, and the
second reads as the first. The declared set is what makes the difference legible.

It is an artifact, not a disposition: written down, reviewed, versioned, and consulted at routing
time. And it **moves in both directions**.

- An oracle question becomes an instrument question when an instrument is built that genuinely
  reaches it, per Part XIV. The set shrinks, and the shrinkage is an achievement worth recording.
- An instrument question becomes an oracle question when an instrument is found to have been
  fabricating — answering against a criterion it had authored. The set grows, and this direction is
  the one nobody looks for, because a working instrument is not usually re-examined for standing.

## 7. Standing and constituency

Not any human is an oracle for a given question. The oracle is whoever bears the consequence, or
holds the authority the question concerns.

> **An oracle answering outside their constituency is just another instrument** — and an
> uncalibrated one.

This matters because "ask a human" in practice means "ask the nearest human". A colleague estimating
what a consumer would prefer is not an oracle for that question; they are a proxy measurement with
unrecorded fidelity, per Part II §7, and their answer will be recorded as a judgment rather than as
a proxy. That is fabricated standing, and it is harder to detect than a fabricated measurement
because it arrives with a name attached.

Two consequences. Every oracle question records **who** answered and **what standing they held**,
so a later reader can assess the answer rather than merely receive it. And where the constituent is
unavailable — a future maintainer, an unreached consumer — the honest record is a proxy answer
labelled as one, not an oracle answer with a substitute signatory.

## 8. Durability

An oracle answer is expensive and must not be re-purchased. Re-asking a settled question is
exhaustion by a different route.

An answer is durable when recorded with the question as asked, the artifact it concerned, the answer,
the answerer and their standing, the date, and its **scope of validity**.

The last field is the one that makes durability honest rather than permanent. Oracle answers have
horizons in Part V §6's exact sense: a judgment that something is good enough was made about a
context, and contexts move. An answer recorded without a scope is either re-asked constantly, which
exhausts the channel, or treated as permanent, which means the system is governed by a judgment about
a situation that no longer exists.

Part XVII owns the routing and recording mechanics in full.

## 9. Boundary

The oracle boundary does not apply to prohibitions and invariants, which admit no judgment. It does
not apply to questions with formal answers, where a bound exists and consulting a person substitutes
opinion for fact.

And one more, which is the failure mode of a Part like this one taken too seriously:

> **Do not route a question outward to avoid a decision you have standing to make.**

Abdication is easily dressed as humility, and it is expensive in the same currency as over-asking.
A system with the standing, the evidence and the criterion to decide, that consults anyway, has
converted its own competence into someone else's workload while appearing careful.

## 10. Evidence — oracle surfaces in this stack

| Surface | What is reserved | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| Rotation of a detected credential reserved to the Owner | An **action** | Reserved decision |
| Stalled-session governor: advisory only, never auto-kill | An **action** | Reserved decision |
| Owner-facing queue | Items needing attention | A channel with no admission criterion |
| Push withheld pending the Owner's word, this session | An **action** | Reserved decision |
| Design review verdicts | A judgment, made by the system against its own criteria | Not reserved — self-answered |
| Whether a floor's value is right | A judgment, per Part XI | Not reserved, not declared, not asked |

**The finding: this stack declares owner-reserved *decisions*, and does not declare owner-required
*judgments*.** These are different objects. A reserved decision says the system may not *act* without
the Owner — credential rotation, killing a session, pushing. A required judgment says the system may
not *conclude* without the Owner — whether a criterion is the right criterion, whether a threshold is
acceptable, whether an artifact is good enough for the person who will use it.

The reserved-decision list is disciplined and well-populated, and it is a safety boundary rather than
an epistemic one. The epistemic boundary — the declared set §6 requires — does not exist here. Every
quality judgment in the stack is self-answered, which is precisely the observation Part I opened
with, now located in its proper place: not a defect in any gate, but an **absent declaration** of
which judgments were never the stack's to make.

The owner-facing queue is the natural host and currently has no admission criterion. Part VII §6
supplied half of one — frontier orderings that turn on value are oracle questions. This Part supplies
the other half: the four marks. Together they specify what belongs in that queue, which is the gap
the family charter recorded and did not fill. — Reserved decisions and queue behaviour OBSERVED from
the governance archive; the decision-versus-judgment distinction is INFERRED against §4.

## 11. Failure modes

| Failure | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Self-certification** | The system answers a question it has no standing to answer; the output is indistinguishable from a measurement |
| **Oracle exhaustion** | Mechanical questions consume the channel until every answer, including the necessary ones, is given carelessly |
| **Feedback-tuned boundary** | Placement adjusted by complaints, which only arrive from over-asking, so the boundary drifts narrow |
| **Nearest-human oracle** | An answer taken from whoever is available rather than from the constituency, recorded as judgment rather than proxy |
| **Approval confused with judgment** | Permission recorded, judgment never captured |
| **Scopeless answer** | Recorded without validity scope, so it is either re-asked forever or obeyed permanently |
| **Abdication** | Questions routed outward that the system had standing, evidence and criteria to answer |
| **Undeclared boundary** | No declared set, so incapacity is indistinguishable from coverage |

## 12. Detection signatures

1. **The quality claim with no oracle in its history.** An artifact declared good with every judgment
   in its record made by the system. Either the boundary is empty or it was never consulted.
2. **The rapid answer.** Oracle questions returned immediately and uniformly. A channel giving
   considered judgments does not have a constant response time; exhaustion does.
3. **The substitute signatory.** Answers about a constituency recorded from someone outside it, with
   no proxy label.
4. **The complaint-shaped boundary.** A history of the boundary moving inward after objections and
   never outward. §5's drift, visible in its own change log.
5. **The permanent judgment.** A recorded answer older than the context it was made about, still
   governing.

## 13. Trap seeds — for Part XXII

- **T-CLAE-SELF-CERTIFICATION** — the system concludes on a question whose criterion it authored,
  producing output indistinguishable from a measurement.
- **T-CLAE-ORACLE-EXHAUSTION** — mechanical questions routed to a scarce judgment channel until the
  genuine questions receive the same degraded attention.
- **T-CLAE-DRIFT-NARROW** — the boundary tuned by complaints, which arrive only from over-asking, so
  it moves reliably toward self-certification.
- **T-CLAE-NEAREST-HUMAN-ORACLE** — an answer taken from whoever was available rather than from the
  constituency, and recorded as a judgment rather than as a proxy with unrecorded fidelity.
- **T-CLAE-ABDICATION** — decisions the system had standing to make routed outward, converting its
  own competence into someone else's workload while appearing careful.

## 14. Rule seeds — for Part XXIII

- **PR-CLAE-DECLARE-THE-BOUNDARY** — the set of properties the system cannot verify about itself is
  written, versioned and consulted at routing time. An undeclared boundary is indistinguishable from
  coverage.
- **PR-CLAE-FOUR-MARKS** — a question is routed outward when it is value-laden,
  constituency-dependent, reference-absent by nature, or self-referential. Absent all four it is an
  instrument question and is answered by a procedure.
- **PR-CLAE-STANDING-RECORDED** — every oracle answer records the answerer and the standing they
  held. Answers from outside the constituency are labelled proxy, not judgment.
- **PR-CLAE-BOUNDARY-NOT-TUNED-BY-COMPLAINT** — boundary placement is reviewed deliberately. Movement
  inward requires the same justification as movement outward, since only one direction generates
  feedback.
- **PR-CLAE-SCOPE-EVERY-ANSWER** — an oracle answer records its scope of validity. Scopeless answers
  are either re-purchased indefinitely or obeyed after their context has gone.
- **PR-CLAE-NO-ABDICATION** — a question the system has the standing, evidence and criterion to
  answer is answered, and the answer is recorded. Routing it outward is a cost, not a courtesy.

## 15. Eval seeds — for Part XXIV

- **Boundary-existence probe.** Look for the declared set. Its absence is the finding, and it is the
  most common state.
- **Judgment-provenance probe.** For a sample of quality claims, identify who answered each judgment
  in the chain. Claims with no oracle anywhere in their history are self-certified.
- **Mark-classification probe.** Take the questions currently routed outward and classify each
  against the four marks. Those matching none are exhausting the channel and should be
  instrumented.
- **Constituency probe.** For each oracle answer, compare the answerer against the constituency the
  question concerned. Mismatches without a proxy label are fabricated standing.
- **Boundary-movement probe.** Examine the declared set's history. Movement only inward, following
  objections, is §5's drift.
- **Answer-age probe.** List oracle answers whose scope of validity has lapsed and which still govern.

## 16. Production Reality Gate seed — for Part XXV

**Oracle Boundary Gate.** A quality claim about an artifact may be published only when the declared
boundary exists and has been consulted, every judgment in the claim's chain that matches one of the
four marks carries an oracle answer with a recorded answerer, standing and scope, and no answer in
that chain is past its validity scope. Claims whose chains are entirely self-answered are published
as self-certified — a label, not a block, so the distinction appears in the ordinary record rather
than requiring an audit.

## 17. Pseudoflow — placing and using the boundary

Write the declared set before it is needed. For each dimension the system makes claims about, ask
whether the criterion is one the system would have to author. Where it is, that dimension's
acceptability judgment belongs outside, and the set records it with the reason.

At routing time, classify the question against the four marks. Value-laden, constituency-dependent,
reference-absent by nature, or self-referential — any one routes it outward. None of the four means
a procedure should answer it, and routing it outward anyway spends a channel that cannot be
replenished.

Identify the constituency before identifying an answerer. The oracle is whoever bears the consequence
or holds the authority the question concerns. Where that party is unavailable, record the answer you
can obtain as a proxy with its fidelity, never as a judgment with a substitute signatory.

Record the answer with its question, artifact, answerer, standing, date and scope of validity. The
scope is what prevents both re-purchasing and permanence.

Review the boundary on a schedule rather than on complaint. Ask in both directions: which oracle
questions now have an instrument that genuinely reaches them, and which instrument questions are
answering against a criterion the system authored. The second direction is the one nobody looks for,
and it is where self-certification re-enters after having been removed.

Before routing anything outward, apply the abdication check: does the system have the standing, the
evidence and the criterion to answer this? If so, answer it and record the answer.

## 18. Integration

Part XVII owns routing, presentation and the mechanics of making an answer durable, and inherits §7's
constituency requirement and §8's scope field. Part XIX's evidence-gated autonomy uses the declared
boundary as the specification of what autonomy may *not* conclude — the complement of what it may do.
Part VII §6's value-laden frontier decisions are the largest single source of oracle traffic, and
together with §3's marks they constitute the owner-facing queue's missing admission criterion. Part
XIV §10 and Part VI §9 both route their residues here.

Outside the family, the reserved-decision rules are endorsed unchanged as a safety boundary and
explicitly distinguished from the epistemic boundary this Part requires. The owner-facing queue is
identified as the host for the declared set's traffic.

## 19. Open questions

1. Can the four marks be applied without already knowing the answer? Determining that a question is
   value-laden may require enough engagement with it to have formed a view, at which point the
   routing is contaminated by the view. — UNKNOWN.
2. What is the correct review cadence for the boundary, given that only one direction of error
   generates feedback? §5 establishes that complaint-driven review drifts narrow; a schedule fixes
   the cadence and not the attention, and attention is what the inward-drift direction lacks. —
   UNKNOWN.
3. Is a proxy answer with recorded fidelity genuinely better than no answer? §7 permits it, and a
   labelled proxy may still be consumed as a judgment by anyone reading past the label — the same
   failure Part II §6 identified for counterfeit distance. — HYPOTHESIS.

## 20. Institutional writeback

Five trap seeds, six process-rule seeds, six eval seeds and one production gate.

Three portable results. **The boundary fails in both directions and only one failure is visible** —
over-asking generates complaints, self-certification generates nothing, so any boundary tuned by
feedback drifts toward the failure that is Part I. **An oracle answering outside their constituency
is just another instrument**, which makes "ask a human" insufficient as a remedy and "ask the nearest
human" a way of fabricating standing that is harder to detect than fabricating a measurement,
because it arrives with a name attached.

And the finding that generalizes furthest: **an organization can have a disciplined list of
owner-reserved decisions and no declared boundary at all.** Reserved decisions govern what the system
may *do*; the declared boundary governs what it may *conclude*. The first is a safety mechanism and
the second is an epistemic one, and having the first is no evidence of the second — which is why a
stack with this much governance still answers every quality judgment itself.
